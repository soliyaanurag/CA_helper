# GST registration: who must register and when

## The turnover limits
You must register for GST when your **aggregate turnover in a financial year** crosses the limit that applies to you:

| What you supply | Most states | Some special-category states |
|---|---|---|
| Only goods (where the state has adopted the higher limit) | ₹40 lakh | ₹20 lakh |
| Services, or goods and services together | ₹20 lakh | ₹10 lakh |

The lower limits apply in certain north-eastern and hill states, and not every state adopted the ₹40 lakh limit for goods. **Check the limit for your own state on the GST portal before relying on it.**

## What counts as "aggregate turnover"?
It is the value of all your supplies across India under the same PAN: taxable, exempt and inter-state supplies and exports. It does **not** include the GST itself or supplies on which the buyer pays tax under reverse charge.

## When you must register even below the limit
Some businesses must register **whatever their turnover** (section 24). The common cases are:
- making **inter-state** taxable supplies of goods (some services and handicraft goods have exceptions)
- being liable to pay tax under **reverse charge**
- selling through an **e-commerce operator** that collects tax at source (some exceptions apply)
- casual taxable persons and non-resident taxable persons
- input service distributors, and persons required to deduct or collect tax under GST

## How and when to register
- Apply online on the GST portal within **30 days** of becoming liable. Registration is free.
- You receive a 15-character **GSTIN**. It starts with your state code and contains your PAN.
- You can also register **voluntarily** below the limit. This lets you claim input tax credit and sell to GST-registered buyers, but you must then file all GST returns.

## In CA Helper
If your turnover is above the limit and you are not registered, your profile shows **"may need GST registration"**. This is a prompt to check with a CA; it is not a legal ruling.
