# GST composition scheme: eligibility, rates and restrictions

## What is it?
A simpler GST option for small businesses. You pay tax at a **low fixed rate on turnover** and file fewer returns, but you give up input tax credit and some freedoms.

## Who can opt in?
| Type of business | Turnover limit (previous financial year) |
|---|---|
| Manufacturers, traders, restaurants (not serving alcohol) | ₹1.5 crore (₹75 lakh in certain special-category states) |
| Service providers, and mixed suppliers (separate composition option) | ₹50 lakh |

The limit is checked on **aggregate turnover** across all registrations under the same PAN.

## Tax rates (total of CGST + SGST, on turnover)
- Manufacturers and traders: **1%**
- Restaurants not serving alcohol: **5%**
- Service providers under the separate option: **6%**

## Restrictions
- **No input tax credit** on purchases.
- **You cannot charge GST to customers.** You issue a *bill of supply*, not a tax invoice.
- **No inter-state sales of goods.** You can buy from other states, but you cannot sell goods outside your state.
- **Not allowed for certain goods**, e.g. ice cream, pan masala and tobacco products (the list is notified).
- You must mention **"composition taxable person"** on your signboard and bills.

## Opting in and out
- **New registration:** choose composition in the registration application.
- **Existing regular taxpayer:** file **Form CMP-02** on the GST portal before the start of the financial year.
- **Leaving the scheme:** file **CMP-04** to opt out voluntarily. If your turnover crosses the limit during the year, you move out of the scheme and must inform the department promptly; check the current timeline on the portal.

## Returns under composition
You file **CMP-08** every quarter to pay tax and **GSTR-4** once a year. See *gst-cmp-08-and-gstr-4.md*.

## Is it right for you?
It suits small businesses that sell mainly to consumers within their state and have few purchases with GST. If you sell to GST-registered businesses, or have large purchases with GST, the regular scheme may work out better. That's a good question for a CA.
