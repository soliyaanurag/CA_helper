# Input tax credit (ITC): basic conditions and time limits

## What is input tax credit?
When you buy goods or services for your business, you pay GST to the supplier. **Input tax credit** lets a regular GST taxpayer reduce the GST payable on sales by the GST already paid on business purchases.

## Conditions: all must be met
1. You have a valid **tax invoice** or debit note.
2. You have actually **received** the goods or services.
3. The invoice appears in your **GSTR-2B**, meaning the supplier has reported it in their GSTR-1 or IFF (see *gst-gstr-2b.md*).
4. The supplier has actually **paid the tax** to the government.
5. You have **filed your GSTR-3B**.

## Pay your supplier within 180 days
If you do not pay the supplier (value plus tax) within **180 days of the invoice date**, you must **reverse** the credit with interest. You can claim it again once you pay.

## Time limit to claim
ITC for invoices of a financial year must be claimed by **30 November of the next financial year**, or the date you file the annual return for that year, **whichever is earlier**.

## Credit you cannot claim (blocked credits)
Some purchases do not give ITC even when used in business. Common examples are most motor vehicles for passenger transport (with exceptions, e.g. for transport businesses), food and beverages, club memberships, and goods or services for personal use. The full list is in section 17(5); check it before claiming.

## Who cannot claim ITC at all?
Businesses under the **composition scheme** cannot claim input tax credit.
