# TDS basics: who must deduct, TAN, and depositing TDS

## What is TDS?
**Tax Deducted at Source**: when you make certain payments (salary, contractor bills, professional fees, rent...), you **deduct a part as tax**, pay the rest to the payee, and **deposit the deducted tax with the government** in the payee's name. The payee later claims credit for it in their own return.

## Who has to deduct TDS?
- **Companies, firms and LLPs** making payments covered by TDS rules, above the limits for each type of payment.
- **Employers** paying salary above the taxable limit.
- **Individuals and HUFs** running a business or profession generally have to deduct TDS on payments such as contractor fees, professional fees and rent **only if their accounts were subject to tax audit in the previous year**.
- Some rules apply to **all individuals**, audit or not. For example, individuals paying **rent above ₹50,000 per month** deduct TDS using their PAN, through a simple challan-cum-statement, without a TAN. Check the current rules for your case.

## TAN: your TDS account number
- A **TAN** (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) is a 10-character number needed to deduct and deposit TDS and to file TDS statements.
- Apply online (Form 49B) through the Protean (NSDL) TIN website or the income tax e-filing portal.
- Cases that use a challan-cum-statement with PAN, such as the rent case above, don't need a TAN.

## Depositing TDS
- Deposit the TDS by the **7th of the following month**. For tax deducted in **March, the deadline is 30 April**.
- **Interest** applies if you don't deduct when required (1% per month) or deduct but deposit late (1.5% per month).

## Under the new Income-tax Act, 2025 (from 1 April 2026)
TDS rules now sit in **section 392** (salary) and **section 393** (other payments), and challans use new payment codes. Rates and limits were largely carried over. Payments made up to 31 March 2026 still follow the old section numbers.
