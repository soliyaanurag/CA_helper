# GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B: what they are and when they are due

## The two main returns of a regular GST taxpayer
- **GSTR-1** is the statement of your **sales** (outward supplies), invoice by invoice for B2B sales and in summary for B2C sales. Your buyers' input tax credit depends on it, because your GSTR-1 feeds their GSTR-2B.
- **GSTR-3B** is the **summary return where you pay tax**. You declare your total sales and tax, claim eligible input tax credit and pay the balance. It must be filed **even when there was no business** in the period (a nil return).

## Due dates
| Filing type | GSTR-1 | GSTR-3B | Tax payment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly** filers | 11th of the next month | 20th of the next month | With GSTR-3B |
| **Quarterly (QRMP)** filers | 13th of the month after the quarter | 22nd or 24th of the month after the quarter, depending on your state | First two months of the quarter: by the 25th of the next month through challan PMT-06; third month: with GSTR-3B |

QRMP (Quarterly Return, Monthly Payment) is **optional** for taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹5 crore. See *gst-iff-qrmp.md* for details. Which states file on the 22nd and which on the 24th is set by notification; the GST portal shows your due date.

## Things that changed recently
- **Three-year limit:** since July 2025 the GST portal does not accept a return filed more than **three years after its due date**. Old pending returns should be cleared early.
- **Auto-filled tax cannot be edited in GSTR-3B:** the tax liability that flows from GSTR-1 into GSTR-3B is locked. Mistakes must be corrected in GSTR-1 (or GSTR-1A) *before* filing GSTR-3B.

## If you are late
- A **late fee** applies for every day of delay, and **interest at 18% a year** applies to tax paid late. See *gst-late-fee-and-interest.md*.
- You cannot file GSTR-3B for a later period until the earlier one is filed.
