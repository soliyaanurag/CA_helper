# GSTR-1: what it is and why it matters

<!-- Reviewed on 29 Sep 2026 against the official pages listed at the bottom (V1). Content rule
     (content/README.md): no legal thresholds, rates or due dates here; the app shows them from the
     rule tables. -->

## What is it?
GSTR-1 is the GST return in which you report your **sales**: every invoice you issued in the period, the credit and debit notes, and a summary by product or service (HSN/SAC code). Your customers' tax credit depends on it: what you report here appears in their own GST statements (GSTR-2B).

## Who has to file it?
Businesses registered under the regular GST scheme. The app shows it when your profile says "Regular" GST. Businesses in the composition scheme do not file it (they file CMP-08 and GSTR-4).

## How often, and for which period?
Monthly, or quarterly if you are in the QRMP scheme (your profile shows which). Each return covers the sales of that month or quarter. In the QRMP scheme you may also report sales to registered buyers in the first two months of a quarter through the optional Invoice Furnishing Facility (IFF), so they get their credit sooner. The due date is shown on this page and in your calendar.

## What happens if it is late or skipped?
A late GSTR-1 brings a late fee, and your customers may not see the tax credit for your invoices. Returns are filed in order: the GST portal does not accept a GSTR-1 while an earlier GSTR-1 or GSTR-3B is missing, and GSTR-3B for a period cannot be filed before its GSTR-1.

## Filing it yourself vs. with a CA
Filing it yourself works well when you have a small number of invoices and keep them in order. A CA helps when you have many invoices, exports, credit notes or amendments to earlier periods.
