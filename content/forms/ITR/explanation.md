# Income tax return (ITR): what it is and why it matters

## What is it?
The income tax return reports the **income of your business for the financial year** and the tax on it. Which ITR form you use (ITR-3, 4, 5 or 6) depends on your type of business and your income; your profile shows the right one. From tax year 2026-27 (the Income-tax Act, 2025) new ITR forms are notified under the Income-tax Rules, 2026.

## Who has to file it?
Every business and self-employed person. The form and the due date depend on your profile, for example whether a tax audit applies (section 44AB of the 1961 Act; section 63 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 from tax year 2026-27). The due date is later for audited accounts, and also depends on your type of business.

## How often, and for which period?
Once a year, after the financial year ends. The due date is shown on this page and in your calendar; it is later when your accounts are audited.

## What happens if it is late or skipped?
A late return brings a late fee and interest on unpaid tax, and a business loss can be carried forward to later years only if the return was filed on time.

## Filing it yourself vs. with a CA
Filing it yourself is reasonable for the presumptive scheme (ITR-4; sections 44AD and 44ADA of the 1961 Act, section 58 of the 2025 Act from tax year 2026-27) with simple income. A CA helps with books of accounts, an audit, capital gains or a firm or company return.
